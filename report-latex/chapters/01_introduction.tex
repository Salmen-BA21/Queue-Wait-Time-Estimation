\section{Context of the Project}

\subsection{Introduction}
In a context where digital transformation and artificial intelligence are increasingly shaping operational decision-making, service organizations are under growing pressure to improve waiting experience and service efficiency. Supermarkets, banks, and service centers all face a common challenge: queues are dynamic, difficult to monitor manually, and highly sensitive to demand fluctuations.

In practice, customers often join a queue without a clear idea of expected waiting time, while staff and managers lack real-time indicators to adapt resources quickly. This uncertainty can lead to customer dissatisfaction, reduced perceived service quality, and inefficient allocation of human resources during peak periods.

Conventional approaches remain limited. Manual observation is time-consuming and subjective, simple counters do not capture queue dynamics, and many solutions provide only point estimates without confidence levels. As a result, decisions are often made using incomplete information, especially in crowded or rapidly changing situations.

This is the context in which this end-of-studies project was carried out with DataDoIt. The objective is to design and implement an intelligent, real-time queue monitoring system that combines computer vision, queueing theory, and Bayesian uncertainty quantification. The system detects and tracks people from video streams, estimates arrival and service rates, computes expected waiting time, and provides confidence intervals to support more reliable operational decisions.

\subsection{Presentation of the Project}

\subsubsection{Project Context}
This project focuses on real-time queue monitoring from video streams in order to transform raw visual data into operational indicators for decision support.

\subsubsection{Problem Statement}
Manual approaches or simple counting-based methods remain limited:
\begin{itemize}
    \item they are time-consuming,
    \item they do not explicitly estimate waiting time,
    \item they do not report uncertainty in the measurements.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Study of Existing Approaches}

\paragraph{Study of Existing and Similar Solutions}
Existing solutions are generally based on manual counting, basic counters, or isolated analytics components that do not provide a full end-to-end queue estimation pipeline.

\paragraph{Limitations of Existing Solutions}
Most existing approaches either ignore uncertainty, provide delayed analytics, or fail to combine detection, tracking, queue modeling, and actionable alerting in one integrated system.

\subsubsection{Proposed Solution}
The proposed system combines person detection, multi-object tracking, zone-based filtering, queue-theory metrics, and Bayesian uncertainty quantification with dashboard and webhook integration.

\subsubsection{Objectives of the Project}
This work aims to design a system able to:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item detect and track people in real time,
    \item count customers inside a configurable queue zone,
    \item estimate arrival rate $\lambda$ and service rate $\mu$,
    \item compute expected waiting time $W$,
    \item quantify uncertainty for key metrics,
    \item provide a monitoring interface and external integration (n8n).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Target Audience}
The main audience includes operational staff (for real-time supervision), managers (for resource planning), and end-users who benefit from reduced waiting time and improved service flow.

\subsection{Project Management Methodology}

\subsubsection{Choice of Methodology: Agile}
The project follows an incremental and iterative approach to deliver functional modules progressively while integrating feedback and resolving technical risks early.

\subsubsection{SCRUM}
Development was organized in explicit Scrum sprints with incremental deliverables: Sprint 1 (core perception pipeline), Sprint 2 (GUI and multi-video support), Sprint 3 (YOLO26 migration and performance tuning), Sprint 4 (uncertainty quantification), and Sprint 5 (n8n integration and alerting).
