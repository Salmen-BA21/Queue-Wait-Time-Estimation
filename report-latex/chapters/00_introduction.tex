\chapter*{General Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{General Introduction}

In a context where digital transformation and artificial intelligence increasingly shape operational decision-making, service organizations are under strong pressure to reduce waiting time and improve service quality. Supermarkets, banks, and service centers face the same recurring challenge: queues are dynamic, difficult to monitor manually, and highly sensitive to demand fluctuations.

In practice, customers often join a queue without a clear idea of expected waiting time, while managers and operational staff do not always have reliable real-time indicators to adapt resources quickly. Manual observation remains subjective and costly, while simple counting solutions do not provide robust waiting-time estimation with confidence levels.

This end-of-studies project addresses this gap through the design and development of an intelligent real-time queue monitoring system based on computer vision and queueing theory, with explicit uncertainty quantification. The proposed system detects and tracks people from video streams, analyzes occupancy inside configurable queue zones, estimates arrival and service rates, computes waiting time, and exposes confidence-aware metrics for operational use.

This work combines classical queueing theory with modern computer-vision detection and tracking techniques 
\cite{gross_queueing,ultralytics_docs} to provide actionable waiting-time estimates with quantified uncertainty.

This report is organized as follows. Chapter 1 presents the context of the project, host organization, problem statement, and objectives. Chapter 2 introduces the state of the art. Chapter 3 details the methodology and system design. Chapter 4 presents implementation and development through sprint-based delivery. Chapter 5 reports results and evaluation. Chapter 6 discusses contributions and limitations. Chapter 7 concludes the work and outlines future directions.
