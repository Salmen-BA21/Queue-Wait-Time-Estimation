\chapter*{General Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{General Introduction}

Service organizations are under constant pressure to reduce waiting time and improve service quality. Supermarkets, banks, and service centers all face the same problem: queues change quickly, are difficult to monitor by hand, and react strongly to demand fluctuations.

In practice, customers often join a queue without knowing how long they will wait, while managers and operational staff rarely have reliable real-time indicators for adjusting resources quickly. Manual observation is subjective, and simple counting tools are not enough to estimate waiting time in a meaningful way.

This end-of-studies project addresses that gap through the design and development of a real-time queue monitoring system based on computer vision and queueing theory. The proposed system detects and tracks people in video streams, analyzes occupancy inside configurable queue zones, estimates arrival and service rates, computes waiting time, and exposes metrics that can be used operationally.

It combines classical queueing theory with computer-vision detection and tracking techniques 
\cite{gross_queueing,ultralytics_docs} to provide actionable waiting-time estimates.

This report is organized as follows. Chapter 1 presents the project context, host organization, problem statement, and objectives. Chapter 2 defines the requirements analysis and the main architecture choices. Chapters 3 to 9 follow the implementation sprint by sprint, from the perception foundations to evaluation and optimization. Chapter 10 concludes the report and presents future work directions.
